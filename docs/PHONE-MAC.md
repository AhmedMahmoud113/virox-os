# Switch off the phone's "private Wi-Fi address" for your home network

New phones may use a different device number (MAC) on each network, and sometimes change it. The
router then thinks the phone is a new device: its limits and its line do not apply, and its usage is
counted on another device. The surest fix: the phone uses its real number on your home network only.
This changes nothing on other networks.

## Android (Samsung and others)

1. Open **Settings**, then **Connections** (or **Network & internet**), then **Wi-Fi**.
2. Tap the gear icon next to your home network's name.
3. Tap **View more** or **Advanced**.
4. Under **MAC address type** (or **Privacy**), choose **Phone MAC** (or **Use device MAC**).
5. The phone disconnects and comes back within seconds.

## iPhone

1. Open **Settings**, then **Wi-Fi**.
2. Tap the (i) icon next to your home network's name.
3. Switch off **Private Wi-Fi Address** (or set it to **Off**).
4. Tap **Continue** if a message appears.

## After that

- If the phone shows up once as a new device: open **Devices**, tap the new device, then **More →
  This is the same device as**, and choose the old phone. Its usage and settings move to it.
- Instead, you can give each person a **personal Wi-Fi password** (**Wi-Fi → Personal
  passwords**): the router then knows whose phone it is from the password, even if its number changes.
